    <h3 style="font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 700; color: #111827; margin-top: 0; margin-bottom: 0.75rem; border: none;">Thermodynamic Waste Heat Optimization in GreenVolt Systems</h3>
    <p style="font-size: 0.95rem; color: #4b5563; line-height: 1.6; margin-bottom: 1.25rem;">
      <strong>Abstract:</strong> Educational infrastructure generates substantial unsegregated solid waste while incurring high climate-control energy costs. GreenVolt is an integrated waste-to-energy recovery system combining computer-vision automated classification with on-site aerobic composting and thermoelectric heat harvesting. Primary sorting utilizes optical, infrared, and mass sensors to maximize recycling purity. Exothermic heat from organic decomposition is captured via phase-change storage and Seebeck-effect thermoelectric generators (TEGs). Thermodynamic modeling indicates an annual carbon offset of 2,460 kg CO₂e against an operational footprint of 1,396 kg CO₂e (a 176% net-positive offset ratio). Deployment yields a 30–40% reduction in landfill waste, a 25% improvement in sorting efficiency, and a 10–15% offset in local power demands.

    <p style="font-size: 0.95rem; color: #4b5563; line-height: 1.6; margin-bottom: 1.25rem;">
      <strong>Abstract:</strong> Agricultural byproduct biofilters offer a low-cost, decentralized alternative to energy-intensive water purification systems. By utilizing semi-charred sunflower seed husks, banana peels, and coconut husks, this research evaluates adsorption kinetics and surface complexation mechanisms for heavy metal (Cu²⁺) removal and turbidity reduction. Using Langmuir adsorption isotherm models, empirical results demonstrate removal efficiencies up to 95% while reducing operating costs to $0.02–$0.10 per m³ of water treated.
